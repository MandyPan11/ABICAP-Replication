# Original Article Example: The Kidneys

## Passage

The kidneys are a pair of bean-shaped organs just above the waist (KC1). 
They have many functions in the body (KC2), including producing hormones (KC3), absorbing minerals (KC4), filtering blood (KC5), and producing urine (KC6).
Urine is the liquid waste product of the body (KC7). 
Wastes in the blood come from the normal breakdown of tissues, such as muscles, and from food (KC8). 
After the body has taken the nutrients it needs from food, some of the wastes are absorbed into the blood (KC9). 
If the kidneys did not remove them, they would build up in the blood and damage the body (KC10). 
The actual removal of wastes from the blood occurs in tiny units inside the kidneys called nephrons (KC11).
The kidneys secrete hormones that help maintain homeostasis (KC12). 
Erythropoietin is a hormone that stimulates bone marrow to produce red blood cells when more are needed (KC13). 
They also secrete the hormone renin, which regulates blood pressure (KC14), and calcitriol, the active form of vitamin D, which helps maintain calcium for bones (KC15). 
The kidneys themselves are also regulated by hormones (KC16). Antidiuretic hormone from the hypothalamus stimulates the kidneys to produce more concentrated urine when the body is low on water (KC17).
The kidneys are involved in maintaining the water level in the body (KC18), regulating red blood cell levels (KC19), and regulating blood pressure (KC20). 
They react to changes in the body’s water level throughout the day (KC21). 
As water intake decreases, the kidneys adjust accordingly and leave water in the body instead of helping remove it through the urine (KC22). 
The kidneys need constant pressure to filter the blood (KC23). 
When blood pressure drops too low, the kidneys increase the pressure by producing angiotensin (KC24), a blood vessel-constricting protein (KC25).
